What else the site needs

Beyond the two policies — ranked by whether it actually matters

DO IT  Accessibility statement

A short page saying you're committed to accessible design and giving a phone number and email for anyone who has trouble using the site. Costs you fifteen minutes. ADA website suits against small businesses are common and a good-faith statement plus a working contact route is meaningful protection. It's also just correct — some captains are ordering from a phone in bad light on a moving boat.

DO IT  A real footer, on every page

Legal name, mailing address, phone, email, and links to both policies. Absent-footer is one of the strongest "is this business real?" signals people read without noticing. For a service that asks barge lines to route invoices to you, looking established is worth more than it costs.

DO IT  A link to the Terms on the order form

One line above the submit button:

By placing this order you agree to our Terms of Service and Privacy Policy.

Don't add a checkbox. A required tick is one more thing between a captain and a submitted order, and for B2B terms a clear notice at the point of submission is generally sufficient. Friction on that button costs you real orders.

SKIP  A cookie consent banner

You don't run advertising or third-party analytics cookies. A banner would imply you do, annoy every user, and protect you from nothing. If you ever add Google Analytics, revisit this — that's the trigger, not the website itself.

SKIP FOR NOW  A formal service agreement

Different document, different purpose: the Terms cover anyone who uses the site, while a service agreement covers a specific barge line's rates and payment terms. Ingram's account is worth having one eventually. It isn't a launch blocker, and it's a lawyer's job rather than a template's.

WORTH A THOUGHT  Insurance certificate on request

Not a web page — a note in your back pocket. Barge lines vetting a new vendor frequently ask for a COI. Being able to produce one within the hour is a credibility signal, and not having one ready is the kind of thing that stalls an account for two weeks.
