# Grafton Towboat Services — Launch Film
### Treatment & shooting script

---

## The idea

**"Same form. Everything else, new."**

Every customer you have knows the paper order form by heart — creased, highlighted,
handwritten in the margins. So we open there. Not to mock it, but because it's the
most recognizable object in your customers' working lives. Then we set the pen down.

The film is not a feature tour. It's one cook, one order, one delivery — and the app
appears the way it actually appears in their day: in a galley, at odd hours, on a
phone, with the river outside the window.

**Positioning line (Jen's, and it's the real one):**
*Nobody on this stretch of river has anything like this.* That's a category claim,
not a feature claim. The film should feel like the arrival of something, not the
advertising of something.

---

## Tone

Confident and quiet. Think equipment-brand film — Yeti, Filson, John Deere — not
tech-startup. Working hands, real light, real weather. No stock footage, no
whooshes, no upbeat ukulele. The product is convenience; the *feeling* is respect
for the people doing the work.

**Do:** natural sound (river, gulls, engine hum, pen on paper, crate on steel deck)
**Don't:** screen-recording montages set to synth music, spinning phone mockups, emoji

---

## Format

| Cut | Length | Use |
|---|---|---|
| **Hero** | 75–90 sec | Facebook/site header, showing to barge lines, sales meetings |
| **Social cutdown** | 30 sec | Facebook/Instagram feed |
| **Silent loop** | 15 sec | Autoplay with captions — most people scroll muted |

Shoot once, cut three times. Everything below is the hero; the cutdowns are lifts.

## Shooting script — hero cut

### 1. The form (0:00–0:12)

**Visual:** Extreme close-up. Coffee ring on paper. A yellow highlighter drags across
a line. A pen circles a quantity. Fingers smooth a crease. We never see a face yet.
**Sound:** Pen scratch. Paper. Distant tow engine.
**Text on screen:** `For years, this is how the river ordered groceries.`

> Shoot on an actual used form — the more marked-up, the better. Ask Sinclair's for
> one that's already been shopped.

### 2. The turn (0:12–0:20)

**Visual:** The pen is set down on the form. A hand picks up a phone instead.
Over-the-shoulder, galley window behind, river light.
**Sound:** Pen down. A beat of quiet.
**Text:** `Not anymore.`

### 3. The same list (0:20–0:32)

**Visual:** Screen recording, held in hand, real galley lighting. The **Barge Order
Form** view — scrolling past *Beef*, *Pork*, *Poultry*, the same section headers as
the paper. Tapping a couple of items.
**Text:** `The same order form you've always used.`

> The point lands only if a customer recognizes the section names. Scroll slowly
> enough to read them.

### 4. …and the rest of the store (0:32–0:42)

**Visual:** Thumb slides the toggle to **Full Sinclair's Store**. Grid fills with
products and photos. Search: type "coffee" — results appear instantly.
**Text:** `Plus everything else Sinclair's carries.`
**Text (small):** `Search works even when your signal doesn't.`

### 5. Order again (0:42–0:52)

**Visual:** Account → order history. Last trip's order. One tap: **Reorder**. Cart
fills. A quick edit — bump a quantity, remove one line.
**Text:** `Last trip's order, again — in one tap.`

> This is the strongest feature for a cook who orders the same staples every run.
> Give it room.

### 6. Crew CODs (0:52–1:02)

**Visual:** An item switched to **COD**, a crew member's name typed in, Venmo
selected. Then the order summary showing the boat's total and the crew member's
total as separate lines.
**Text:** `Personal items, kept separate. Never on the boat's bill.`

### 7. Sent (1:02–1:10)

**Visual:** Review & submit. Confirmation. Cut to phone face-down on the galley
table; cook pours a coffee, looks out at the water.
**Text:** `Sent.`

### 8. Delivered (1:10–1:25)

**Visual:** Dawn. The refrigerated van. Crates coming down to the dock. A hand-off.
Boat easing into Grafton, Mile Marker 219. Cook checking crates. A nod between two
people who've done this a hundred times.
**Sound:** Engine, water, crate on steel, brief laugh.
**Text:** `Boat or land. Refrigerated the whole way.`

### 9. End card (1:25–1:35)

**Visual:** Black or deep green. Logo.
**Text:**
```
GRAFTON TOWBOAT SERVICES
Order from the galley. We'll meet you at the dock.

graftontowboatservices.com
(618) 556-0290
```

---

## 30-second cutdown

Sections 1 → 2 → 3 → 5 → 8 → 9.
Drop the store toggle, COD, and the send beat. Reorder and the delivery are the
emotional core; everything else is detail that belongs in the hero cut.

---

## 15-second silent loop

Paper form → pen down → app scroll → **Reorder** tap → crates on the dock → end card.
Captions burned in. No audio dependency.

---

## Shot list (what to actually capture)

**At Sinclair's**
- A used, marked-up paper order form (close-ups: highlighter, pen, creases)
- Store aisles, the deli case, fresh meat counter
- Optional and strong: a shopper working the barcode pick sheet at the register

**On or near a boat**
- Galley interior — table, window, coffee
- Hands on a phone, over-the-shoulder, natural light
- Exterior: boat approaching, tie-up, dock

**Delivery**
- Refrigerated van, doors opening
- Crates: frozen / refrigerated / shelf
- The hand-off, the signed slip, a check of the load

**Screen recordings** (record at full brightness, clean status bar, real order data)
1. Barge order form scroll showing section headers
2. Toggle to full store + search "coffee"
3. Order history → Reorder → edit a line
4. COD: name + Venmo
5. Review & submit → confirmation

---

## Practical notes

- **Shoot the screen twice:** once as a clean screen recording, once as a phone held
  in a real hand in real light. Cut between them — the held shots sell it, the clean
  recordings read clearly.
- **Use a real order**, not lorem-ipsum products. Ground chuck, coffee, eggs, paper
  towels. Cooks will notice.
- **Golden hour for the delivery.** Dawn on the river is the shot that makes this
  look like a $100K film, and it costs nothing but an early alarm.
- **Get a release** from anyone whose face appears — driver, cook, Sinclair's staff.
- **Vertical safe:** frame with headroom so a 9:16 crop works for Facebook/Instagram.

---

## Music

One track, restrained. Acoustic or light Americana with a slow build — nothing that
peaks. It should feel like morning, not like a launch. The delivery sequence is the
only place it lifts.

---

## Lines worth using

- *Order from the galley. We'll meet you at the dock.* ← recommended tagline
- *Same order form. Everything else, new.*
- *The river's grocery order, finally caught up.*
- *Everything Sinclair's carries. Delivered to the boat.*

---

## Who says what

If you want a voice, use **Jen** — she's the one who's been telling barge lines this
for years, and a real voice from the company beats a hired read. Keep it to two or
three lines over the delivery sequence. Otherwise let text and natural sound carry it.
